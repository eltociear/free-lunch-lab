  4. 2011–2013

    NGO / research

    Madhya Pradesh cash transfer pilots

    Madhya Pradesh, India

    Run by
    SEWA (Self-Employed Women’s Association), funded by UNICEF
    What was paid
    Small monthly cash to every man, woman and child in eight randomly chosen villages from June 2011 to November 2012 (Rs 200 per adult and Rs 100 per child, later Rs 300 and Rs 150), with 12 control villages; plus one tribal village for 12 months
    Result
    Girls’ normal weight-for-age improved more than twice as much as in control villages; 29.0% of recipient households reported higher school attendance (13.5% without); a shift from wage labour to own work; less debt. Village-level comparison reported by the pilot team (2015 book)
    Source
    library.oapen.org

  9. 2018–2025

    NGO / research

    Baby's First Years

    New York City, New Orleans, the Twin Cities and Omaha, USA

    Run by
    A university research team funded by the National Institutes of Health and private foundations, with payments on a debit card

  10. 2019–2021

    NGO / research

    Stockton Economic Empowerment Demonstration (SEED)

    Stockton, California, USA

    Run by
    Stockton Economic Empowerment Demonstration (SEED), started by then-Mayor Michael Tubbs and paid for by private donors and foundations, not the city budget
    What was paid
    $500 a month with no conditions for 24 months (February 2019–January 2021) to 125 randomly selected residents of Stockton's lower-income neighborhoods (the study assigned 131 people to receive it and 200 to a control group)

    Philanthropic
    Result
    A peer-reviewed randomized trial (Journal of Urban Health, 2023; 131 treatment, 200 control) reported lower income volatility (19% vs 26%), less psychological distress and better energy and physical functioning than the control group in the first year.   16. 2020–2023

    NGO / research

    OpenResearch Unconditional Cash Study

    Illinois and Texas, USA

    Run by
    OpenResearch, a nonprofit research lab, with economists including Eva Vivalt, Elizabeth Rhodes, Alexander Bartik, David Broockman, Patrick Krause and Sarah Miller
    What was paid
    $1,000 a month with no conditions for three years (November 2020–October 2023) to 1,000 low-income people aged 21–40 in 19 counties of Illinois and Texas; 2,000 others were randomly assigned to a control group that got $50 a month
    Funding
    Philanthropic
    Result
    The researchers (NBER working paper, July 2024; published in the Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2026) reported, vs the control group: income excluding the transfers fell by about $1,900 a year, recipients worked 1–2 fewer hours a week, mostly taking more leisure, and spending rose broadly; there was no measurable gain in job quality, and higher well-being in year one faded afterward
    Source
    openresearchlab.org · nber.org

  36. 2022–

    Government

    Basic Income for the Arts (Ireland)

    Ireland

    Run by
    Government of Ireland (Department of Culture, Communications and Sport)
    What was paid
    €325 a week, taxable, for three years (from September 2022, later extended to February 2026) to 2,000 artists and arts workers picked at random from about 8,200 eligible applicants; another 1,000 formed a control group. Made permanent in 2026: 2,000 new places drew just over 10,000 applicants

Estimated social return: €1.39 per €1 of public money

    Run by
    Government of the Marshall Islands; paid out by the Marshall Islands Social Security Administration (MISSA)

    About US$200 every quarter (about $800 a year) to every Marshallese citizen living in the country, children included, paid from the Trust Fund for the People of the Republic of the Marshall Islands, set up under the Compact of Free Association with the US (US military access in exchange for aid); the US carried out 67 nuclear tests here in 1946–1958. The FY2027 budget proposes $32.5 million a year to keep payments near $200. Under the 2023 Compact renewal the US pays $200 million a year into the fund for 2024–2026 and $100 million in 2027, with no US payments scheduled after that; the fund held about $1.3 billion in mid-2025. The IMF advised replacing the universal payment with a targeted one and raised concerns about the USDM1 digital asset

Newcomers are paid only after 90 days of verified residence, with three months back-paid. Boseong adds 50,000 won from its own funds (200,000 won in total) and Boeun 10,000 won
    Result
    The ministry says the population of the first 10 counties rose 4.7% after the pilot began, and local-currency merchants 13.7%. Okcheon gained 1,491 new residents in December 2025 (290 left), and Cheongsong 318 in the week after it was selected. Okcheon must find about 53 billion won over two years, roughly 30% of its budget, and has suspended other programs. BIEN says this version does not meet its definition, a periodic cash payment to all; the pilot is limited to some counties and paid in local vouchers
    Source
